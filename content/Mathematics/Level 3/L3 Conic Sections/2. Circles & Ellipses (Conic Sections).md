# Ellipses
An ellipse is defined as the set of all points in a plane where the sum of the distances from any point on the curve to two fixed points (called **foci**) is constant.

The definition states that the sum of the distances from a point to both foci is constant. This means that in the diagram, the distance of the blue line + distance of the red line = distance of the purple line + distance of the green line.

Now, lets try to form the equation of the ellipse using this definition!
We know that

$$
\sqrt{ (x-c)^{2}+y^{2} }+\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }=(a-c)+(a+c)
$$


$$
\underbrace{ \sqrt{ (x-c)^{2}+y^{2} }+\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} } }_{ \text{Sum of distances from foci to }(x,y) }=\underbrace{ (a-c)+(a+c) }_{ \text{Sum of distances from }(a,0) }
$$

You can do any other point on the curve, but using the distance from $(a,0)$ makes the calculation easier since it is the $x$-intercept (the zero in the equation makes things easier 😁)

$$
\implies \sqrt{ (x-c)^{2}+y^{2} }+\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }=2a
$$

Now we can do some algebraic manipulation to simplify the equation.


$$
\sqrt{ (x-c)^{2}+y^{2} }=2a-\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }
$$


$$
(x-c)^{2}+y^{2}=4a^{2}-4a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }+(x+c)^{2}+y^{2}
$$


$$
\cancel{ x^{2} }-2cx+\cancel{ c^{2} }+\cancel{ y^{2} }=4a^{2}-4a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }+\cancel{ x^{2} }+2cx+\cancel{ c^{2} }+\cancel{ y^{2} }
$$


$$
\implies-2cx=4a^{2}-4a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }+2cx
$$


$$
-4cx=4a^{2}-4a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }
$$


$$
-4cx-4a^{2}=-4a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }
$$


$$
cx+a^{2}=a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }
$$


$$
\left[cx+a^{2}=a\sqrt{ (x+c)^{2}+y^{2} }\right]^{2}
$$


$$
(a^{2}+cx)^{2}=a^{2}\left[(x+c)^{2}+y^{2}\right]
$$


$$
a^4+2a^{2}cx+c^{2}x^{2}=a^{2}\left[x^{2}+2cx+c^{2}+y^{2}\right]
$$


$$
a^4+\cancel{ 2a^{2}cx }+c^{2}x^{2}=a^{2}x^{2}+\cancel{ 2a^{2}cx }+a^{2}c^{2}+a^{2}y^{2}
$$


$$
a^4+c^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}c^{2}-a^{2}y^{2}=0
$$


$$
(c^{2}-a^{2})x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2}+a^4-a^{2}c^{2}=0
$$

Now we can use the identity $c^{2}+b^{2}=a^{2}$. If you're wondering where this identity came from, check the ellipse diagram then apply Pythagoras (or just check the formula sheet since it also says there)


$$
\implies -b^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2}+b^{2}a^{2}=0
$$

Now divide all sides by $a^{2}b^{2}$

$$
\implies \boxed{\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1}
$$

..which is an equivalent expression to the one on the formula sheet!

# Circles
Circles are pretty much the same thing as ellipses except it's a but squished. 

In the above derived formula of ellipse

$$
\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1
$$

If $a\equiv b$ then it's a circle. If $a\neq b$, then it's an ellipse.

Do circles have a foci? Yes, a foci of a circle is the center of the circle.
