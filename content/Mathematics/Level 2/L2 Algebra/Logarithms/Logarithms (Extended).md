#L2MAEL 

---

The practice excellence questions on my website have some questions where there are two or more logarithm terms, and they all have different bases.

Here is how the base change rule works.

Suppose we have the following function

$$
y=\log _{b}a \iff b^y=a
$$

Now take the logarithm* of both sides

$$
\log b^y=\log a
$$

$$
y\cdot \log b=\log a
$$

$$
\implies y=\frac{\log a}{\log b}
$$

Since $y=\log_{b}a$ hence:

$$
\boxed{\log_{b}a= \frac{\log a}{\log b}}
$$

\* You can take ANY logarithm; it does not have to be a common log with a base 10 ($\log_{10}$). It can even be natural log with a base $e$ ($\log_{e}$) or  even $\log_{2}$. **Use the base of a logarithm that makes the calculation easier.** 

Note: When the base of the logarithm is equal to the argument, it is equal to 1. (e.g. $\log_{10}10=1$, $\log_{2}2=1$, $\log_{5}5=1$, $\log_{e}e \equiv \ln e=1$, etc...)
