# What is **equilibrium**

- Equilibrium $(Kc)$ is to keep the $system$ balance and stability  

$$
Kc = \frac{P}{R}
$$  
$$
R = Reactant
$$  
$$
P = Product
$$  
$$
R \rightleftharpoons P
$$
> - $R \rightleftharpoons P$ called $System$ or $Reaction$
    - If this question is an equilibriumquestion, you must use $\rightleftharpoons$

---

# Write the equilibrium constant expression

- The equilibrium constant expression is

$$
Kc = \frac{[P]}{[R]}
$$

> [!question] Example question  
> Write the equilibrium constant expression for  
> $$
> H_{2}CO_{3}(aq) + H_{2}O(ℓ) \rightleftharpoons HCO_{3}^{-}(aq) + H_{3}O^{+}(aq)
> $$

We have to know which one is the reactant and which one is the product

$$
H_{2}CO_{3}(aq) + H_{2}O(ℓ)\space \text{ is Reactant}
$$  
$$
HCO_{3}^{-}(aq) + H_{3}O^{+}(aq)\space \text{ is Product}
$$

> [!important] Answer  
> Therefore, the equilibrium constant expression is:  
> $$
> Kc = \frac{[HCO_{3}^{-}]\space \space [ H_{3}O^{+}]}{[H_{2}CO_{3}]\space \space [H_{2}O]}
> $$


> [!danger] Be Careful
 > Remember to keep the charge in the equilibrium constant expression

> If the component has a coefficient, eg:  
> $$
2HCO_{3}^{-}
$$
> The coefficient number will become the power of the component  
> $$
[HCO_{3}^{-}]^{2}
$$

## 2. Indicates the ratio of products and reactants

> - You will get equilibrium constant $(Kc)$ in this question(sometimes you don’t)
> - They could say “there are more reactants than products” or “more products than reactants”

> [!question] Example question  
> The value of the equilibrium constant is $4.30 × 10^{-7}$  
> Explain what this indicates about the ratio of products and reactants

Look at the magnitude of $Kc$, $4.30 × 10^{-7}$ is a really small number because it has a $10^{-7}$  
$$
Kc = \frac{P}{R}
$$  
$Kc$ is small; therefore, the reactant is present in larger amounts. The concentration of R is larger than the concentration of P

Because  
$$
\frac{P}{R\uparrow}\space = \space Kc\downarrow 
$$

> [!danger] Be Careful  
> In this question, you MUST write $Kc = \frac{P}{R}$ to explain

# Describe forward and reverse reactions and explain the value of $Kc$

You usually get two things that they are asking:

> Describe the effect of temperature on the forward and reverse reactions
> 
> Explain any changes that would occur to the value of $Kc$

> [!question] Example question:
> 
> Global oceanic temperatures have been increasing since the Industrial Revolution.
> 
> Predict the impact on the position of equilibrium as temperature increases, if the enthalpy of this reaction is $ΔH = –3.85 \space kJ\space mol^{-1}$
> 
>  - Describe the effect of temperature on the forward and reverse reactions
>  - Explain any changes that would occur to the value of $K_{c}$

> Enthalpy: means the energy state of a system or reactant / product

Firstly, from the question that we can know this is EXOTHERMIC because the environment temperature has been increased $ΔH$ is also negative, means reaction released heat energy into the area

> [!important] Answer - Part one:  
> The enthalpy of this reaction is a negative value, showing this reaction is exothermic. This means the reverse must be endothemic. Increasing the temperature will favour the endothermic reaction, so the reverse reaction is favoured.

> [!danger] Memorize  
> Increasing the temperature will favour the endothermic reaction, so the reverse reaction is favoured
> OR
> Increasing the temperature will favour the exdothermic reaction, so the reverse reaction is favoured


$$
\text{Reactant} \rightleftharpoons \text{Product} + \text{Heat (Temperature)}
$$

$$
R \uparrow \ \rightleftharpoons \ P+T\uparrow 
$$

> [!important] Answer - Part two:  
> Kc is ONLY affected by temperature, so by favouring the reverse reaction, because reactants increase (more products are converted into reactants), Kc will get smaller.

> [!danger] Memorize  
> System favours($\leftarrow$) forwards($\to$) / reverse reaction, $Kc$ will get smaller / bigger, more reactants / products will be produced
